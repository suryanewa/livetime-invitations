# Asset conventions and provenance manifest

Owner: design.inc. Every mesh, texture, font, environment and sound used at runtime is listed here with creator/source, licence, modifications, source master and runtime derivative (Master §8).

## Conventions

- **Axes and units.** Y-up, right-handed, ground plane at y = 0. One scene unit is a "stage decimetre": objects are stylised, not real-world scale. A ready-pose object spans roughly 2–2.5 units so the shared camera frames every family consistently.
- **Transforms.** Applied transforms; origins at the natural contact point (base centre for grounded objects, clip pivot for the card). Pivots are named nodes, never implicit mesh origins.
- **Named nodes.** Each scene exports exactly the node names in its sub-spec (e.g. `business_root`, `card_authored`, `card_secondary`, `clip_pivot` …). Authored motion writes `*_authored`; bounded secondary springs write only `*_secondary` children. No node is written by two systems.
- **Materials.** `mat_<role>[_<preset>]`, resolved at runtime from the selected direction's material library, so presets and art direction change values, not geometry. Directions A, B and L use roughness bands: satin 0.40–0.55, enamel 0.62–0.75, metal 0.25–0.40, food 0.55–0.86. The shipping direction L2 (style reference v1.1, `DIRECTIONS.L2` in `src/render/materials.ts`) no longer follows those bands where the premium pass asked for gloss: chrome `mat_metal` 0.14, bezel 0.18, dark metal 0.20, anodised coral 0.26, cloche 0.30 and brushed platter 0.32 (both anisotropic); satin bodies 0.40–0.42 under a light clearcoat (0.25–0.3); glass faces and the reader pad 0.24–0.30 with clearcoat 1 / 0.06–0.08; card laminates 0.45 with clearcoat 1 / 0.1; cobalt stand 0.38 with clearcoat 0.8; glazes 0.18–0.22 and frosting 0.48–0.55 with sheen. Enamel inserts keep L's values (coral 0.62, cobalt 0.66). Metalness is 0 or 1 except explicitly anodised or painted metal and L2's holographic foil (0.85).
- **Status materials** are unique instances (`mat_status`) so indicator colour never touches card or body materials.
- **Signature detail.** UVs on signature surfaces map one UV unit to one tile in scene units, so stipple or perforation density is identical on every object.
- **Contact shadows** are named `<object>_shadow`, move with their object, and fade and spread with height.
- **Shadow flags.** Dense meshes set `userData.noShadowCast` (in Birthday v1.2: the joined frosting, the stand and the candle wax; in Dinner the plate and cloche; in v1.1 the piped cake body, drip glaze, pearls, the turned stand and its band, candles and wicks); a low-poly stand-in with `userData.shadowProxy` casts their real-time shadow and is invisible in the main pass (`models/shadowProxy.ts`). Both flags travel in the GLB as node extras, and `applyShadowProxies` re-applies the proxy material after loading.
- **Posters.** WebP per scene × visually distinct preset × {host-ready, guest-ready, accepted, declined}, 720², quality 0.86, same framing/background as the renderer, generated from the browser renderer. Scenes whose declined pose hides the preset difference (Party, Birthday) share one declined poster; Dinner (v1.2, one look) has one shared set for every pose. Only the currently needed poster is fetched. Since v1.3 posters and thumbnails are rendered under the production studio rig (`DEFAULT_RIG`, `neutral-room`) through the perspective lens; a comparison rig chosen with `?rig=` on a local host shows no poster.
- **Stage colour.** The stage colour is a property of the studio rig: its rendered backdrop, measured as the mean sRGB of the central 20 % of the empty stage (`pnpm capture stagecolor <baseUrl> <rig>`) and declared as the rig's `stageColor`, which the Stage paints as the scene background. The CSS token `--lt-stage` (`src/ui/shell.css`) equals the production rig's colour, so poster, fallback, canvas, poster cross-fade and collection-thumbnail fill agree; `tests/rigs.test.ts` holds both. Since v1.3 it is **`#D5D2CD`** (`neutral-room`; v1.1–v1.2: `#F8E4C8`, the plaster as rendered). A change of production rig means re-measuring, then regenerating the posters and thumbnails (`pnpm capture <scene>`). No CSS finish sits over the stage since v1.3.
- **Sizes** below were measured from the files in `assets/` on 2026-09-30, after the final captures (v1.2 rows: 2026-10-01, after the v1.2.1/v1.2.2 polish captures; v1.3 figures: 2026-10-01, at `40f9e04`, after the studio-pass captures): raw is `ls -l`, gz is `gzip -9c <file> | wc -c` (1 KiB = 1024 bytes; poster and thumbnail sizes in KB = 1000 bytes). The export report printed by `pnpm models` compresses with Node's zlib at level 9, which gives a few percent more than macOS `gzip -9` at the same level, so its gzip figure is a little larger than the gz column here (Sports 6159.4 vs 5915.7 KiB).
- **Runtime binding.** GLB materials are named placeholders; `bindLibraryMaterials` re-binds them from the locked library at load, so tokens change without re-exporting geometry.
- **Masters.** Through v1.1, models were code-authored parametric sources in `models/` (see below). Since v1.2 the invitation objects are the scan-to-CAD rebuilds in `models/masters/` (rows below; generators, tools and process notes in the repository `github.com/suryanewa/scan-to-cad-rebuilds`, private), imported into each scene contract by a recipe in `scripts/masters/recipes/`; the builders keep the parts that stay code-authored (Sports stand, Birthday wicks and flames, Party tonearm rest, contract skeletons, the shared bin), and the runtime rigs keep the strap, status tile, contact shadows and wisps. Every export is deterministic (same code, same GLB). Selected high-resolution exports and approved style frames go to `~/Creative/Livetime/invitation-interactions/`.

## Why code-authored masters

This machine has no Blender installation, and the handoff transfers no DCC files. The models are therefore authored as explicit, parametric TypeScript builders: lathe profiles with per-corner fillets, bevelled extrusions, rounded boxes and fixed-length strap curves; since the premium pass also SDF housings (surface nets, decimated at export) and profile sweeps along plan paths. Each builder is the editable master. It is original work, has no third-party geometry, and exports deterministically to GLB from Milestone 2 onward. Primitive-looking parts are placeholders only where the study labels them "blockout". This substitution needs Surya's visual approval at the style-selection gate (Master §5: "do not substitute generic primitive assemblies … without visual approval"). **v1.2 (2026-10-01):** at Surya's request the invitation objects themselves are now the scan-to-CAD rebuilds (original design.inc geometry, rebuilt as parametric/PBR models with Meshy image-to-3D scans used as references only); the parts listed above stay code-authored.
