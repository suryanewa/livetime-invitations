# Asset conventions and provenance manifest

Owner: design.inc. Every mesh, texture, font, environment and sound used at runtime is listed here with creator/source, licence, modifications, source master and runtime derivative (Master §8).

## Conventions

- **Axes and units.** Y-up, right-handed, ground plane at y = 0. One scene unit is a "stage decimetre": objects are stylised, not real-world scale. A ready-pose object spans roughly 2–2.5 units so the shared camera frames every family consistently.
- **Transforms.** Applied transforms; origins at the natural contact point (base centre for grounded objects, clip pivot for the card). Pivots are named nodes, never implicit mesh origins.
- **Named nodes.** Each scene exports exactly the node names in its sub-spec (e.g. `business_root`, `card_authored`, `card_secondary`, `clip_pivot` …). Authored motion writes `*_authored`; bounded secondary springs write only `*_secondary` children. No node is written by two systems.
- **Materials.** `mat_<role>[_<preset>]`, resolved at runtime from the selected direction's material library, so presets and art direction change values, not geometry. Directions A, B and L use roughness bands: satin 0.40–0.55, enamel 0.62–0.75, metal 0.25–0.40, food 0.55–0.86. The shipping direction L2 (style reference v1.1, `DIRECTIONS.L2` in `src/render/materials.ts`) no longer follows those bands where the premium pass asked for gloss: chrome `mat_metal` 0.14, bezel 0.18, dark metal 0.20, anodised coral 0.26, cloche 0.30 and brushed platter 0.32 (both anisotropic); satin bodies 0.40–0.42 under a light clearcoat (0.25–0.3); glass faces and the reader pad 0.24–0.30 with clearcoat 1 / 0.06–0.08; card laminates 0.45 with clearcoat 1 / 0.1; cobalt stand 0.38 with clearcoat 0.8; glazes 0.18–0.22 and frosting 0.48–0.55 with sheen. Enamel inserts keep L's values (coral 0.62, cobalt 0.66). Metalness is 0 or 1 except explicitly anodised or painted metal and L2's holographic foil (0.85).
- **Status materials** are unique instances (`mat_status`) so indicator colour never touches card or body materials.
- **Signature detail.** UVs on signature surfaces map one UV unit to one tile in scene units, so stipple or perforation density is identical on every object.
- **Contact shadows** are named `<object>_shadow`, move with their object, and fade and spread with height.
- **Shadow flags.** Dense meshes set `userData.noShadowCast` (in Birthday: the piped cake body, drip glaze, pearls, the turned stand and its band, candles and wicks); a low-poly stand-in with `userData.shadowProxy` casts their real-time shadow and is invisible in the main pass (`models/shadowProxy.ts`). Both flags travel in the GLB as node extras, and `applyShadowProxies` re-applies the proxy material after loading.
- **Posters.** WebP per scene × visually distinct preset × {host-ready, guest-ready, accepted, declined}, 720², quality 0.86, same framing/background as the renderer, generated from the browser renderer. Scenes whose declined pose hides the preset difference (Party, Birthday) share one declined poster; Dinner (v1.2, one look) has one shared set for every pose. Only the currently needed poster is fetched.
- **Sizes** below were measured from the files in `assets/` on 2026-09-30, after the final captures: raw is `ls -l`, gz is `gzip -9c <file> | wc -c` (1 KiB = 1024 bytes; poster and thumbnail sizes in KB = 1000 bytes).
- **Runtime binding.** GLB materials are named placeholders; `bindLibraryMaterials` re-binds them from the locked library at load, so tokens change without re-exporting geometry.
- **Masters.** Models are code-authored parametric sources in `models/` (see below). Since v1.2 the invitation objects are the scan-to-CAD rebuilds in `models/masters/` (rows below), imported into each scene contract by a recipe in `scripts/masters/recipes/`; the builders keep the parts that stay code-authored. Selected high-resolution exports and approved style frames go to `~/Creative/Livetime/invitation-interactions/`.

## Why code-authored masters

This machine has no Blender installation, and the handoff transfers no DCC files. The models are therefore authored as explicit, parametric TypeScript builders: lathe profiles with per-corner fillets, bevelled extrusions, rounded boxes and fixed-length strap curves; since the premium pass also SDF housings (surface nets, decimated at export) and profile sweeps along plan paths. Each builder is the editable master. It is original work, has no third-party geometry, and exports deterministically to GLB from Milestone 2 onward. Primitive-looking parts are placeholders only where the study labels them "blockout". This substitution needs Surya's visual approval at the style-selection gate (Master §5: "do not substitute generic primitive assemblies … without visual approval").
